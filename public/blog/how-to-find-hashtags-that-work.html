How to Find Hashtags That Actually Work (and Which to Skip)

October 23, 2026 · 5 min read

Pasting thirty popular hashtags under every post does not work. The biggest hashtags move so fast that your post disappears in seconds, and platforms increasingly rely on your caption, audio and video content to decide who should see it. Hashtags still help, but only when they are chosen with care.

Use three kinds of hashtags

A balanced set usually includes one or two from each group:

  • Broad and viral. Large tags with lots of traffic. They give you a small chance at a big audience.
  • Niche-specific. Tags used by people who care about exactly your topic. These bring the viewers most likely to follow you.
  • Evergreen. Tags that stay useful all year, such as the name of your craft or product type.

A handful of well-chosen tags (three to five is a sensible starting point) usually beats a long list. Test and see what your own results say.

How to check a hashtag before you use it

  1. Open its results page. Do the top posts match your topic? If the tag is full of unrelated content, skip it.
  2. Look at the dates. If the newest posts are weeks old, nobody is browsing it.
  3. Look at the quality. If every post is spam, your post will be grouped with spam.
  4. Check that it is allowed. Some tags are restricted or hidden by platforms. If a tag shows few or no results, avoid it.

Put keywords in your caption too

People search by typing words, not only by tapping hashtags. Say what your video is about in plain language in the first line of the caption and, if you speak in the video, say the key phrase out loud. Search on social platforms reads all of it.

Track what works

For two weeks, record the hashtags you used on each post and the views it got in the first day. Look for tags that keep showing up on your better posts. Keep those, drop the rest, and try new ones in their place.
