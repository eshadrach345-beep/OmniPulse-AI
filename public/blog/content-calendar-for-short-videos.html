How to Build a 4-Week Content Calendar for Short Videos

October 23, 2026 · 5 min read

A content calendar is not about posting more. It is about removing the daily question of “what do I post today?” so you can spend your energy making good videos. This four-week plan is simple enough to start this week.

Step 1: Choose three or four content pillars

Pillars are the repeating themes your channel is about. A fitness creator might use workouts, nutrition, mindset and gear reviews. Having pillars means you always know what kind of video fits, and your audience knows what to expect from you.

Step 2: Decide a rhythm you can keep

Consistency beats intensity. Three to five posts a week that you can sustain for months are better than a daily schedule you abandon after two weeks. Be honest about how much time you have.

Step 3: Map the month

  1. Week 1: foundations. Videos that explain who you help and your best-known tips.
  2. Week 2: problem solving. Answer the questions people ask you most.
  3. Week 3: proof. Results, examples, behind-the-scenes and stories.
  4. Week 4: engagement. Opinions, polls, reactions and a recap of the month.

Rotate your pillars through each week so no single theme dominates.

Step 4: Reserve a trend slot

Leave at least one slot a week for something timely. When a topic is moving in your niche, you can use that slot immediately instead of rearranging everything.

Step 5: Batch the work

Pick one day a week for creating. Write scripts for the week, generate or record the videos, add captions and schedule them. When posting day arrives there is nothing left to do except reply to comments.

Step 6: Reuse each video

One idea can become a short video on several platforms, a text post, a carousel and a newsletter paragraph. Change the caption and format, not the core idea.

Step 7: Review every week

Every Sunday, look at your top two and bottom two posts. Ask what the best ones had in common: the topic, the hook, the length, the posting time. Do more of that next week.

A simple template

Write the days across the top of a page and your pillars down the side. Fill each slot with a one-line idea and the hook you will use. That page is your calendar. It takes about thirty minutes and removes weeks of second-guessing.
