How to A/B Test Your Video Hooks (and Know When You Have a Real Winner)

October 23, 2026 · 5 min read

The opening seconds of a video do more work than the rest of it. If you want to improve results, testing your hooks is one of the most reliable places to start. The trick is to test properly so you learn something true.

What an A/B test is

You make two versions of the same post that differ in one thing only, publish both under similar conditions, and compare the results. For hooks, version A and version B share the same video and caption but start with different opening lines.

Change one thing at a time

If you change the hook, the thumbnail and the caption together, you will not know which change mattered. Keep everything else identical. If you want to test the thumbnail next, do it in a separate test.

Keep the conditions fair

  • Post at similar times of day, on the same account or similar accounts.
  • Do not boost one version with ads and not the other.
  • Give both versions the same amount of time before you compare them.

Choose the right measure

Raw views can be misleading. Better signals are how many people watch past the first few seconds, average watch time, and the share of viewers who like, comment or share. Pick one main measure before you start so you are not tempted to choose whichever number looks best afterwards.

Do not trust small differences

If version A got 1,020 views and version B got 980, that is noise, not a winner. The smaller the audience and the closer the numbers, the more likely the gap is just chance. Run the test again, or wait for more data. A clear and repeated gap across several tests is what counts.

Write down what you learn

Keep a simple log: the two hooks, the measure, the result and what you think it means. After ten tests you will see patterns, such as question hooks beating statement hooks for your audience. Reuse what wins.

Hook ideas to test

  • A question versus a bold statement.
  • Starting with the result versus starting with the problem.
  • A number (“3 mistakes”) versus no number.
  • A direct address (“If you sell online…”) versus a general opening.
